14:30 – 15:00  ·  Stop One
白頭蔡肉圓
Baitou Tsai Meat Dumpling
The Vibe
Before heading to the coast, we stop for one of Taichung's most iconic street snacks. 白頭菜肉圓 — crispy on the outside, soft and savory within — is a style of deep-fried pork dumpling that locals have lined up for generations. A small, perfect taste of everyday Taiwanese life.
Highlights
  • A Taichung classic — deep-fried rice dumplings filled with seasoned pork and bamboo shoots.
  • Crispy golden shell with a chewy, tender interior — unlike anything you'd find back home.
  • The perfect fuel before an afternoon walk on the wetlands.

15:30 – 16:00  ·  Stop Two
Taichung Aquarium
台中海洋館
The Vibe
A flexible mid-afternoon option on the coast. If time permits after the meatball stop, the Taichung Aquarium offers a cool, indoor respite — particularly welcome if the weather turns. Home to a variety of marine life native to Taiwan's coastal waters, it's a short and pleasant detour before heading to the wetlands. If rain is heavy and Gaomei is not ideal, the aquarium can also serve as a comfortable alternative to linger a little longer.
Highlights
  • Diverse marine exhibits featuring Taiwan's coastal and tropical species.
  • Fully indoor — a practical shelter option in the event of heavy rainfall at the coast.
  • Conveniently located just 15 minutes from the meatball stop and en route to Gaomei.

16:00 – 17:00  ·  Stop Three
Gaomei Wetlands
高美濕地
The Vibe
One of Taiwan's most magical landscapes. As the afternoon light softens toward golden hour, the shallow waters of Gaomei reflect the sky and the silhouettes of giant wind turbines standing at the edge of the Taiwan Strait. Visitors wade barefoot through the warm, shallow flats as the sun descends — a quiet, almost surreal experience that stays with you long after you leave.
Highlights
  • Wade barefoot through the shallow wetland flats — warm, calm, and unlike any beach experience.
  • Iconic wind turbines lining the coast, dramatic against the setting sun.
  • One of Taiwan's best sunset spots — golden hour here is genuinely spectacular on a clear day.
  • Rich birdlife and coastal ecosystem, protected as a nature reserve.
  • Tip: bring sandals or flip-flops you don't mind getting wet, and a change of socks!

Before You Go
Essential Preparation
& Gear Recommendations
  • Weatherproof Apparel — A raincoat or poncho is strongly recommended for protection against both potential rain and the characteristically strong coastal winds. A wide-brimmed hat with an adjustable chin strap is also advised: it provides sun protection if the weather clears, while staying secure against heavy gusts.
  • Footwear — Waterproof sandals or slippers are highly practical. At the end of the boardwalk lies the "Sustainable Use Zone," where visitors may step into the shallow wetlands. Easily washable, waterproof footwear saves the trouble of repeated shoe changes.
  • Small Towel or Wet Wipes — Recommended for cleaning and drying feet upon returning from the mudflats.
  • Utility Plastic Bags — Useful for storing damp apparel, soiled footwear, or personal waste. Strict anti-littering regulations are enforced throughout the sanctuary to protect the habitat.
  • Hydration — There are no shaded structures or commercial vendors along the boardwalk. Please carry an adequate supply of drinking water for the duration of the walk.
An Overview of the Gaomei Wetlands
Located south of the Dajia River estuary in the Qingshui District of Taichung City, the Gaomei Wetlands encompass a vast expanse of over 700 hectares. This unique geographical setting features a diverse matrix of mudflats and sandy beaches, fostering a highly productive and ecologically significant ecosystem.
  • Rich Ecological Diversity — The sanctuary is renowned for its expansive meadows of Bolboschoenus planiculmis, a rare and endangered plant species in Taiwan that creates a striking visual landscape as it sways in the coastal wind. This habitat supports diverse benthic organisms including mudskippers, fiddler crabs, and various brachyuran species. During the autumn and winter migration seasons, the wetlands attract rare migratory birds — including the endangered Black-faced Spoonbill and the Chinese Egret — making it a premier site for ornithological observation.
  • The Gaomei Boardwalk — Spanning approximately 800 meters, this elevated wooden boardwalk was engineered to balance ecotourism with environmental conservation, allowing visitors to traverse the wetlands and observe dense populations of fiddler crabs and mudskippers without disturbing their fragile habitats.
  • The Gaomei Lighthouse & Wind Turbines — Adjacent to the wetlands stands the historic Gaomei Lighthouse, the only lighthouse in Taiwan with a striking red-and-white horizontal striped facade. A prominent alignment of large-scale wind turbines flanks the landscape. Against a setting sun or misty sky, these structures form a compelling juxtaposition of industrial engineering and natural scenery.
  • The Gaomei Wetlands Landscape Bridge — Taiwan's first double-pylon, curved cable-stayed bridge without intermediate piers, gracefully spanning the Qingshui Drainage Channel. With dedicated lanes for cyclists and pedestrians, the bridge illuminated at dusk integrates seamlessly into the surrounding vistas of wind turbines and wetland silhouettes.
